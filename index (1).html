When anyone can make a song in seconds, the bottleneck is listening.

AI music tools have made creation almost effortless, and new tracks now arrive faster than any team could ever hear them. What’s missing is a fast, fair way to find the songs that deserve a bigger audience, and to do something with them while they’re still rising.

Great songs sink in hours

A new track gets one short window on the home feed. If the right listeners miss it, it’s gone, however good it is.

Play counts are a weak signal

They reward what’s already popular. Replays, saves and remixes say far more about whether a song has something.

A hit doesn’t become a career

Creators with one great track have no clear next step toward distribution, sync or development, so many never make a second one.

Buyers can’t search the flood

Music supervisors, labels and brands need tracks that fit a brief and are cleared to use, not an endless scroll.

      A score only matters if something happens next.

      Ngoma closes the loop from a song to an opportunity, and tells the creator why, so one good track turns into a second one.

      1. Listen

        Opted-in tracks are analyzed within minutes of release: audio features plus the first wave of listener behavior.

      2. Score

        Five signals, weighted for each lane, produce one explainable score from 0 to 100.

      3. Route

        Tracks that cross a lane’s threshold go straight to the destination built for them.

      4. Grow

        Creators see what drove their score and the next best move, and partners get a ranked shortlist instead of raw volume.

      Built to run where the music is made.

      Ngoma works best as a native layer inside a music-making platform. It gives a great first song a next step, and gives creators a reason to stay for the second one.
